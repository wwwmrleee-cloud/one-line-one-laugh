# Candidates — One Line, One Laugh

Gate: 1–140 characters, one line, UTF-8, non-blank. Judge brief (in order):
laugh first (real turn/surprise), then tightness, freshness, clarity.
Playbook micro-text standard: a concrete named person/thing/scene, a twist
in the last third, an unanswered question left hanging, no preaching.

## The eight candidates

| # | chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| A | 68 | My therapist asked what my love language is. I said 'read receipts.' |
| B | 114 | My Roomba filed a workplace complaint. It says I keep moving the furniture and calling it 'character development.' |
| C | 92 | My neighbour's dog barks at me every morning. I've started barking back. He respects me now. |
| D | 85 | My bank app flagged unusual activity. It was me buying groceries instead of takeaway. |
| E | 87 | I told my plants I'm going on holiday. They've started a group chat called 'Survivors.' |
| F | 67 | My smoke alarm cheers every time I cook — it's the only fan I have. |
| G | 95 | I bought a standing desk to be healthier. Now I just sit on the floor next to it, out of spite. |
| H | 108 | I told my smart speaker a secret. Now the ads know it — and honestly, they listen better than my friends do. |

## Simulated pairwise comparison (judge criteria, in order)

- **A vs B**: both land a real turn. A's reversal (read receipts as a love
  language — the symbol of being ignored, presented as affection) is sharper
  and lands earlier; A is also far tighter (68 vs 114). A wins.
- **A vs C**: C's escalation (bark back, earn respect) is absurd and fresh,
  but A's conceptual flip is sharper and A is tighter. A wins by a hair.
- **A vs D**: D's reversal (responsible behaviour flagged as unusual) is clean
  and tight, but A's turn is more surprising — D's punch is guessable once
  "unusual activity" is set up. A wins.
- **A vs E**: E's "Survivors" group chat is a lovely image, but the trope
  underneath (plants die on holiday) is well worn; A's collision is fresher
  in its wording. A wins.
- **A vs F**: F is the tightest (67) and its "only fan" pun lands cleanly at
  the end, but it is a pun, and the judge brief warns against a pun obvious
  from the first word; A's turn is a reversal, not a wordplay. A wins.
- **A vs G**: G's "out of spite" is a fine absurd ending, but its setup
  (standing desk to be healthier) is a familiar observation; A's setup is a
  sharper collision. A wins.
- **A vs H**: H's punch ("ads listen better than my friends") is a mild
  observation stretched over a long setup; A is tighter and turns harder.
  A wins.
- **B vs C/D/E/F/G/H**: B is the strongest of the rest — the Roomba's
  "character development" is the most original single phrase in the set —
  but it loses to A on tightness and on the sharpness of the turn.

## Picked

**A** — `My therapist asked what my love language is. I said 'read receipts.'`
(68 characters, one line.) Concrete named scene (therapist's office), twist
in the last third (the love language is the receipt that proves you're being
ignored), unanswered question left hanging (what did the therapist say to
that?), nothing explained, nothing preached. It also has a tie-break edge:
at 68 bytes it is among the smallest texts in the field.
